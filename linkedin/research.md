# LinkedIn post research — patterns that get engagement

Goal: build a repeatable way to turn your loose ideas into LinkedIn posts
during the job search, without reading as AI-generated (tropes-filtered, same
as resume content — see `research/tropes.md`).

## Source quality note

A first web search turned up mostly SEO/AI-content-mill sites (finallayer,
connectsafely.ai, viralbrain.ai, tonemark.ai, postiv.ai) quoting suspiciously
precise stats ("65% decide from line 1", "hooks under 10 words outperform by
40%", "contrarian hooks outperform by 2.3X"). Treat those exact numbers as
unverified marketing copy, not data. What's below keeps only the parts that (a)
match long-standing, widely-corroborated LinkedIn wisdom, or (b) are directly
observable in a real reference post that performed well in your own niche —
see the example analysis in `references/` for the method (save a post that
worked, then break down structurally why).

## Durable patterns (cross-checked against the reference post)

**Hook is everything.** LinkedIn truncates to roughly the first 200-210
characters before "see more." If line 1-2 don't create a reason to expand, the
rest of the post is invisible. Effective hook types: a specific, surprising
claim; naming the reader's expected reaction and then reframing it; a number
that seems too big/small to be true. Avoid throat-clearing ("I've been thinking
about...", "Excited to share...").

**Short paragraphs, aggressive line breaks.** 1-2 sentences per paragraph.
This isn't a stylistic tic — it controls reading speed on mobile and keeps the
post from looking like a wall of text.

**Specific numbers and named entities beat vague claims.** "18th to 1st in one
generation," "100M messages," "5 years" land harder than "huge jump" or "lots
of usage." Ideas pulled from the inbox should get concrete numbers attached
before drafting, even rough ones.

**Contrarian / paradigm-break framing drives comments.** State the consensus
view in quotes, then rebut it in one line. This is the single most repeatable
structural device in the reference post (three of them, back to back).

**Tie the macro point back to your own proof of work.** The strongest posts
don't stay abstract — they close by grounding the thesis in the author's own
numbers/results. During a job search this matters doubly: every post is
implicitly a credibility signal, so the "proof" beat should point at
something you actually built or shipped — see `profile/experience-bank.md`
and [ideas.md](ideas.md).

**Close on a compressed thesis, not a CTA-begging question.** The reference
post ends with one line ("O jogo não é sobre escolher o modelo") rather than
"What do you think? Comment below!" — the debate-bait is in the contrasted
claims earlier, not solicited explicitly. Feels more confident, less
engagement-farmy.

**No emoji, no bold-word bullet spam, no unicode arrows.** Consistent with
`research/tropes.md` — the same AI-writing-tropes filter used on resumes
applies here. A post that leans on 🚀/💡 bullets or "It's not X, it's Y" reads
as AI-generated and undercuts credibility, which is the opposite of the goal.

## Working template (derived from the reference post)

1. Hook — one bold, specific, verifiable claim. No preamble.
2. Reframe — name the reader's likely reaction, redirect it.
3. Facts — 2-4 sentences, named entities, concrete numbers.
4. The nuance — what the obvious reading misses (builds credibility).
5. 2-3 "conventional wisdom → rebuttal" pairs, each its own short paragraph.
6. Forward-looking implication (optional) — what this means going forward.
7. Pivot question (optional hinge) — bridges to the personal/thesis angle.
8. Proof of work — ground the thesis in something you actually built.
9. Closing one-liner — compressed thesis, no "thoughts below?" CTA.

Not every post needs all 9 beats — steps 6-7 are the most skippable for a
shorter post — but 1, 2, 3, 8, 9 are the load-bearing ones.

## Open questions / to refine over time

- Which language(s) to post in, relative to your target market — if your
  reference posts are in a different language than you plan to post in,
  translate the *structure*, not the phrasing.
- Cadence and which persona: industry commentary vs. build-in-public updates
  on your own projects — decide whether to alternate or focus on one.
- Whether to link out in the post body (algorithm often deprioritizes posts
  with external links) vs. first comment — needs a decision once posting
  cadence starts.
